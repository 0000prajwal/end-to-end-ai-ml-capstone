Natural Language Processing

Natural Language Processing, or NLP, is a field of Artificial Intelligence that focuses on enabling computers to work with human language. Human language is complex because the same word can have different meanings depending on context. NLP systems attempt to understand, process, classify, translate, and generate text.

Text preprocessing is an important part of many NLP projects. Common preprocessing tasks include converting text to lowercase, removing unnecessary characters, tokenization, and handling punctuation. Depending on the problem, stop words may also be removed.

Tokenization divides text into smaller units called tokens. A token may be a word, part of a word, or a character. Modern language models often use subword tokenization because it can handle rare words and unknown terms more effectively.

Traditional NLP methods represent text using numerical features. A bag-of-words representation records how often words appear in a document. TF-IDF improves this approach by giving greater importance to words that are common in a particular document but less common across the complete collection.

Word embeddings represent words as dense numerical vectors. Words with similar meanings may have similar positions in the embedding space. Sentence and document embeddings can represent longer pieces of text and are useful for semantic search.

NLP is used for sentiment analysis, text classification, spam detection, question answering, machine translation, summarization, and information extraction. A customer support system might classify a ticket into payment, delivery, technical, or account categories.

Sentiment analysis attempts to determine the emotional tone of text. A message can be classified as positive, negative, or neutral. More advanced systems may identify emotions such as anger, satisfaction, or frustration.

Named entity recognition identifies important entities in text. Examples include people, organizations, locations, dates, and products. This can be useful for extracting structured information from unstructured documents.

Text classification assigns documents to predefined categories. A model can be trained using labeled examples. The quality of the labels has a significant impact on the performance of the classifier.

Large language models have expanded the capabilities of NLP systems. They can generate text, answer questions, summarize documents, translate languages, and extract structured information. However, they may sometimes generate incorrect information.

Grounding a language model with reliable context can improve the factuality of answers. Retrieval-augmented generation is one method that retrieves relevant documents and provides them to the language model before answer generation.

Prompt design is also important for language model applications. A prompt should clearly describe the task and desired output. Structured formats such as JSON can make model outputs easier to process programmatically.

NLP systems should be evaluated using appropriate metrics. Classification can use accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Text generation may use different evaluation methods depending on the task.

Language data can contain bias, private information, and cultural differences. NLP developers should consider fairness and privacy when building applications. Systems should be tested across different types of users and language patterns.

NLP continues to develop rapidly. Modern systems combine traditional linguistic methods, machine learning, deep learning, embeddings, and large language models. These technologies allow computers to work with human language in increasingly useful ways.
